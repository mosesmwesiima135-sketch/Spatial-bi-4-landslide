How this works as an early warning system

1. Risk knowledge

Susceptibility from slope, wetness, flow accumulation, soils and land cover, learned from the landslide inventory with a spatial Bayesian model.

2. Monitoring and forecasting

Daily satellite and gauge rainfall update the probability for every slope unit, with uncertainty.

3. Dissemination

Alert levels go to the dashboard and by SMS to Red Cross volunteers and village leaders in the affected units only.

4. Response

Runout paths and exposed households guide evacuation to ridge-top safe sites and anticipatory action.

Definitions and data notes
  • Demo data. Terrain, rainfall, landslides, population and model results are simulated to show how the tool would look. The district outline and sub-county positions are approximate, and the two historic events are placed only approximately.
  • Slope unit: a hexagonal mapping unit of about 0.4 km². In production these would be watershed-based slope units built from the DEM.
  • Nowcast probability: the modelled chance of at least one landslide in the unit today, given terrain and antecedent rainfall. Alert levels: green < 5%, yellow 5–15%, orange 15–35%, red ≥ 35%.
  • Runout path: units downstream of orange and red units along D8 flow directions, where debris would travel.
  • Susceptibility: the static, rain-independent likelihood of failure.
  • TWI: Topographic Wetness Index, ln(upslope area / tan slope), a proxy for soil saturation.
